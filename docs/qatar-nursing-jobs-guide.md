# Qatar Nursing Jobs and Licensing Guide for Nigerian Nurses

Prepared for: Nigerian registered nurses seeking Qatar healthcare roles
Target country: Qatar
Primary pathway: Employer-sponsored healthcare employment plus Department of Healthcare Professions licensing
Research date: 31 May 2026

> This guide is practical job-search and relocation preparation material, not legal advice. Qatar healthcare licensing, exam rules, employer hiring requirements, visa procedures, and salary packages can change. Before paying for verification, exams, recruitment services, or travel, verify the current requirements with Qatar's Ministry of Public Health Department of Healthcare Professions, the employer, and official visa channels.

## 1. Executive summary

Qatar is a realistic destination for experienced Nigerian nurses because the country relies heavily on expatriate healthcare professionals and offers tax-free salaries, modern hospitals, accommodation benefits in many packages, annual leave, medical insurance, and international clinical exposure.

The strongest route is:

1. Confirm eligibility as a Registered General Nurse or relevant nursing scope under Qatar's Department of Healthcare Professions (DHP).
2. Prepare documents early: nursing degree/diploma, transcript, home-country nursing licence, experience letters, good standing certificate, passport, CV, and specialty certificates.
3. Complete Primary Source Verification (PSV) through an approved provider such as DataFlow or QuadraBay when required.
4. Prepare for and pass the Qatar Prometric/DHP qualifying exam if required for the nursing scope.
5. Apply directly to major employers and reputable recruiters.
6. Secure a written offer from a Qatar healthcare facility.
7. Employer sponsors the work visa/residence process.
8. Complete medical, biometrics, residence permit, and final onboarding requirements.

The best candidates are nurses with:

- Bachelor of Nursing or recognised nursing diploma;
- valid Nigerian nursing registration/licence;
- at least 2 years post-registration clinical experience;
- strong English communication;
- specialty experience in ICU, emergency, theatre/OR, NICU/PICU, paediatrics, maternity, dialysis, medical-surgical, oncology, mental health, or outpatient/primary care;
- clean documentation and verifiable employment history.

## 2. Qatar nursing licensing: what changed in terminology

You will still hear people say "QCHP licence" or "Qatar Prometric." In practice, Qatar healthcare professional regulation is now handled under the Ministry of Public Health by the **Department of Healthcare Professions (DHP)**.

Use the correct terms:

- Old/common term: QCHP.
- Current regulator: Department of Healthcare Professions (DHP), Ministry of Public Health.
- Verification: Primary Source Verification (PSV), commonly through DataFlow or QuadraBay.
- Exam: DHP/Qatar Prometric qualifying exam, if required for your scope.
- Final right to practise: DHP registration/licence linked to an approved healthcare facility/employer.

## 3. Best pathway for a Nigerian nurse

### Recommended route

The best route is job-offer-first plus licensing preparation:

1. Prepare documents and check DHP eligibility.
2. Apply to Qatar hospitals and approved recruiters.
3. Begin PSV and Prometric preparation early.
4. Secure employer offer.
5. Employer supports DHP facility licensing and visa process.

Why this route is best:

- Qatar work immigration is employer-sponsored.
- Final licensing often needs a facility/employer link.
- Employers and recruiters may guide document sequence and onboarding.
- Applying with a Qatar-ready document file makes interviews and offers faster.

### Alternative route

Some nurses prepare licensing steps before getting an offer:

- PSV/DataFlow;
- Prometric exam preparation and pass result where applicable;
- DHP evaluation if possible under the current portal rules.

This can help, but do not spend money blindly. First confirm your nursing scope, required documents, and whether the employer wants you to start verification before or after offer.

## 4. Core eligibility for Registered General Nurse

Based on Qatar DHP nursing guidance, a Registered General Nurse pathway typically requires:

- Bachelor Degree in Nursing, usually 4 years; or
- Diploma in Nursing, usually 3 years after 12 years of schooling; or
- Associate degree in nursing where the applicant is licensed as a Registered General Nurse in the country of origin/current practice, subject to DHP criteria;
- valid nursing registration/licence from the country of origin/current practice;
- post-registration clinical experience:
  - commonly 2 years after registration/licensing for bachelor or diploma holders;
  - commonly 3 years after registration/licensing for associate degree holders;
- Primary Source Verification report;
- passing certificate for the qualifying exam if required;
- complete DHP evaluation/licensing application.

For Nigerian nurses, prepare evidence from:

- Nursing and Midwifery Council of Nigeria registration/licence;
- degree/diploma certificate;
- transcript;
- NYSC or exemption, if relevant to employment history;
- hospital employment letters;
- continuing professional development certificates;
- good standing certificate, if required;
- passport.

## 5. DHP licensing process

### Step 1: Identify correct scope

Choose the correct nursing scope before paying for verification or exam preparation.

Common scopes may include:

- Registered General Nurse;
- Midwife;
- specialty nurse;
- home care nurse;
- nursing technician/assistant-related categories where applicable.

Do not apply under the wrong scope just because it seems easier. Wrong scope can delay licensing or limit the roles you can legally perform.

### Step 2: Prepare documents

Typical DHP evaluation documents include:

- valid passport copy;
- national ID or current residence ID if applicable;
- recent photo;
- updated CV;
- academic certificates;
- official transcripts;
- valid nursing registration/licence;
- recent work experience certificates with issue date;
- proof of licence covering the claimed years of work experience;
- Primary Source Verification report;
- qualifying exam passing certificate if applicable;
- employer/facility documents when requested.

Documents not in English or Arabic may need official translation. Always check current DHP upload requirements before submitting.

### Step 3: Primary Source Verification

Primary Source Verification confirms that credentials are genuine by checking them with the issuing institutions.

Commonly verified items:

- education;
- nursing licence/registration;
- employment experience;
- good standing;
- professional certificates where relevant.

Important practical advice:

- Use exactly the same name order across passport, licence, certificates, and CV where possible.
- If names differ because of marriage, initials, spelling, or old documents, prepare an affidavit/legal explanation.
- Make sure employment letters include exact dates, title, department/unit, full-time status, and hospital contact details.
- Inform previous employers and schools that DataFlow/verification providers may contact them.

### Step 4: Qualifying exam / Prometric

Qatar DHP uses computer-based qualifying exams for specified healthcare practitioner categories. Registered General Nurse is listed among the scopes required to take a qualifying exam.

For Registered General Nurse, public guidance commonly describes the exam as:

- multiple-choice computer-based exam;
- clinical competency focus;
- often described as 150 MCQs;
- passing score commonly cited around 50 percent for Registered General Nurse;
- exam rules and validity can change, so verify with DHP/Prometric before booking.

Exam preparation should cover:

- fundamentals of nursing;
- adult medical-surgical nursing;
- paediatrics;
- maternity;
- mental health;
- infection prevention and control;
- pharmacology and medication safety;
- emergency care;
- ethics and patient safety;
- nursing leadership and documentation.

### Step 5: DHP evaluation and licence

After PSV and exam evidence are ready, the file is reviewed through DHP's registration/licensing process.

Possible outcomes:

- eligibility/evaluation approval;
- request for additional documents;
- temporary/provisional facility-linked licence where applicable;
- final licence after employer/facility requirements are completed;
- rejection if eligibility, experience, documents, or verification fail.

Do not resign from a current job or travel until the employer and DHP status are clear.

## 6. Qatar visa and work permit process

For foreign nurses, Qatar employment is employer-sponsored.

Typical sequence:

1. Nurse secures written offer from Qatar employer.
2. Employer confirms licensing and onboarding pathway.
3. Employer initiates work visa/entry permit through Qatar authorities.
4. Nurse submits passport copy, certificates, medical documents, photos, and other requested documents.
5. Entry visa is issued.
6. Nurse travels to Qatar.
7. Post-arrival medical screening and biometrics/fingerprinting are completed.
8. Residence Permit/Qatar ID is processed.
9. Employer completes final onboarding and facility licensing requirements.

Important points:

- The employer is the sponsor.
- Do not enter Qatar on a tourist visa to start work.
- Confirm who pays for visa fees, flight, medicals, licensing, DataFlow, Prometric, accommodation, and relocation.
- Get every promise in writing.
- Qatar employers may offer single status or family status depending on grade/salary/policy.

## 7. Salary and benefits expectations

Qatar nursing packages vary by employer, specialty, seniority, and whether the employer is public, private, or home-care.

Common benefits may include:

- tax-free salary;
- furnished accommodation or housing allowance;
- transport allowance or staff transport;
- annual return flight or ticket allowance;
- medical insurance;
- paid annual leave;
- end-of-service benefit;
- training and professional development;
- relocation support for some roles.

Do not rely only on headline salary. Compare total package:

- basic salary;
- accommodation;
- transport;
- shift allowance;
- overtime policy;
- flight tickets;
- contract length;
- probation period;
- annual leave;
- health insurance;
- malpractice/professional cover;
- licensing fees;
- family status.

## 10. CV strategy for Qatar

Use a Qatar-ready nursing CV.

Recommended sections:

1. Name, phone, email, location, nationality.
2. Target role: Registered Nurse - [specialty].
3. Professional summary.
4. Licence and registration status.
5. Clinical experience by hospital/unit.
6. Key competencies.
7. Education.
8. Certifications.
9. DHP/DataFlow/Prometric status.
10. References.

### CV profile example

> Nigerian Registered Nurse with [number]+ years post-registration clinical experience across [specialty/unit]. Experienced in patient assessment, medication administration, infection prevention, documentation, multidisciplinary collaboration, emergency response, and family education. Preparing for Qatar DHP licensing and open to employer-sponsored nursing roles in Qatar.

### CV bullets

Use evidence:

- Managed average daily patient load of [number] in [unit/specialty].
- Delivered safe medication administration and documentation under [hospital protocol/system].
- Supported emergency response for [type of cases].
- Educated patients and families on [condition/discharge care].
- Maintained infection prevention standards and reduced [issue] by [result], if known.
- Precepted junior nurses or student nurses.
- Participated in audits, quality improvement, morbidity/mortality review, or clinical governance.

## 11. Application strategy

Apply in three tracks at the same time:

### Track 1: Direct employers

Apply to:

- HMC;
- Sidra Medicine;
- PHCC;
- Aman Hospital;
- private hospitals and specialist clinics.

### Track 2: Reputable healthcare recruiters

Use recruiters for:

- interview preparation;
- bulk recruitment campaigns;
- licensing guidance;
- relocation support.

Do not sign anything that prevents you from applying directly unless you fully understand the agreement.

### Track 3: Job boards

Use job boards to identify openings, but always check whether the same role exists on the employer's official website.

Weekly application routine:

- 10 direct employer applications;
- 10 job-board applications;
- 5 recruiter contacts;
- 5 LinkedIn messages to nurse recruiters/hospital HR;
- 1 hour Prometric preparation daily;
- document follow-up with schools, employers, and NMCN.

## 13. Interview preparation

Prepare answers for:

- Tell us about your nursing experience.
- Why Qatar?
- Why this hospital?
- What is your specialty strength?
- How do you handle a deteriorating patient?
- How do you manage medication errors or near misses?
- How do you communicate with doctors and families?
- How do you handle infection prevention?
- What is your experience with electronic medical records?
- Are you ready for shift work?
- Have you started DataFlow/Prometric/DHP?
- When can you join?

Clinical scenarios to review:

- sepsis recognition;
- chest pain;
- stroke warning signs;
- paediatric fever;
- obstetric emergency if maternity;
- airway/breathing/circulation;
- medication rights;
- infection prevention bundles;
- pressure injury prevention;
- falls prevention;
- handover using SBAR.

## 15. Offer checklist

Before accepting, confirm in writing:

- employer legal name;
- hospital/facility name;
- job title and nursing scope;
- salary and allowances;
- accommodation or housing allowance;
- transport;
- annual leave;
- annual flight ticket;
- medical insurance;
- malpractice/professional liability cover;
- probation period;
- contract duration;
- notice period;
- shift pattern;
- overtime policy;
- who pays for DataFlow, Prometric, DHP, visa, medicals, flight, and relocation;
- whether family status is available;
- whether the employer will support DHP facility licensing;
- expected start date;
- refund/bond clauses if leaving early.

Avoid offers that:

- require large upfront payment;
- use Gmail/Yahoo/Hotmail for official hospital HR;
- refuse to provide contract details;
- promise licence without DHP process;
- ask for passport original before a verified process;
- pressure immediate payment to secure a slot.

## 16. Practical action plan

1. Choose target nursing specialty.
2. Update Qatar-ready CV.
3. Gather education, licence, transcript, experience letters, passport, and certificates.
4. Check DHP nurse requirements for the exact scope.
5. Start Prometric preparation.
6. Contact previous employers/school so they will respond to verification requests.
7. Apply to HMC, Sidra, PHCC, Aman Hospital, and private hospitals.
8. Register with reputable recruiters but do not pay suspicious fees.
9. Track every application.
10. Once shortlisted, ask employer about DHP licensing, DataFlow, Prometric, and visa support.
11. After offer, verify employer and contract.
12. Complete licensing/visa sequence as instructed by employer and DHP.
