# Japan Pathway Guide for a Senior Engineering and Operations Candidate

Prepared for: Confidential senior engineering and operations candidate
Current location assumption: Nigeria
Target country: Japan
Research date: 31 May 2026

> This guide is practical job-search and relocation preparation material, not legal advice. Japan's immigration rules, employer sponsorship requirements, visa-centre procedures, and language expectations can change. Before applying, verify current requirements with the Immigration Services Agency of Japan, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, the Embassy of Japan in Nigeria, and the Japan Visa Application Centre operated by VFS Global.

## 1. Executive summary

Japan can work for this candidate, but only with a precise strategy. The candidate should not pursue low-level manufacturing or factory work. The right positioning is senior maintenance reliability, technical services, manufacturing excellence, SAP/MRP/CMMS, TPM/IWS, Lean Six Sigma, production planning, and capital-project execution for Japanese or foreign-affiliated industrial employers.

The strongest routes are:

1. **Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services** - the most realistic Japan work status if the candidate secures a Japanese employer sponsor and the job duties clearly require specialised technical or business knowledge.
2. **Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) points route** - strong if the candidate can reach at least 70 points using education, career history, salary, and other criteria.
3. **J-SKIP special highly skilled route** - powerful but only realistic if the Japan salary is very high: JPY 20 million or more for advanced specialised/technical work with a master's degree or 10+ years relevant experience; JPY 40 million or more for advanced business/management activity.
4. **Intra-company transferee** - useful only if the candidate joins a multinational outside Japan and is later transferred to Japan.
5. **Business Manager** - not recommended unless the candidate will establish or manage a real Japan business with sufficient capital, office, and governance.
6. **Specified Skilled Worker (SSW)** - technically available in manufacturing fields, but generally not suitable for this senior profile because it is designed for job-ready skilled labour, requires Japanese/skills tests, and is not the best match for senior operations leadership.

The best immediate plan is a job-offer-first campaign targeting foreign-affiliated companies, Japanese multinationals with English-speaking global teams, pharma/life sciences manufacturing, semiconductor/electronics manufacturing, automotive/industrial manufacturing, and automated logistics/maintenance environments.

## 3. Japan immigration pathway ranking

### Route 1: Engineer / Specialist in Humanities / International Services

This is the main route for professional employment in Japan. It covers activities based on a contract with a public or private organisation in Japan that require specialised skills or knowledge in engineering, natural sciences, humanities, business, economics, sociology, international services, or foreign-culture-based knowledge.

Official examples include mechanical engineering technicians/engineers, translators, designers, private-company language teachers, and marketing workers. For this candidate, the relevant side is the engineering/business/technical knowledge side, not language services.

Core requirements:

- a contract or formal offer from a legitimate organisation in Japan;
- job duties must require specialised technical or professional knowledge;
- duties must match the candidate's education or relevant professional experience;
- university/college graduation or equivalent education, or proof of relevant practical experience;
- for engineering/humanities fields, at least 10 years of practical experience can support eligibility where degree alignment is not straightforward;
- salary must be equal to or higher than Japanese nationals performing comparable work;
- the Japanese sponsor must be stable and able to support the Certificate of Eligibility (COE) application.

Why this is the best practical route:

- It is commonly used for professional company roles.
- It can fit technical services, engineering operations, production systems, SAP/MRP, project management, reliability strategy, manufacturing excellence, and maintenance planning roles.
- It does not require the very high salary of J-SKIP.
- It is more realistic than SSW for a senior profile.

Best job titles for this route:

- Maintenance Reliability Manager
- Technical Services Manager
- Manufacturing Excellence Manager
- Operational Excellence Manager
- Production Planning Manager
- Engineering Operations Manager
- Plant Engineering Manager
- Maintenance Systems / CMMS Manager
- SAP PM / SAP MRP Manufacturing Process Lead
- Project Manager, Manufacturing or Engineering
- CQV / Commissioning / Validation Manager, if the candidate has suitable regulated-industry project evidence
- Facilities / Utilities Maintenance Manager

Critical risk:

- Japan immigration examines the actual job duties, not just the job title. The role must not look like manual factory labour, general supervision, assembly work, or routine technician work. The application should clearly describe strategy, technical analysis, planning, reliability systems, project leadership, engineering knowledge, SAP/MRP/CMMS, and management of technical operations.

### Route 2: Highly Skilled Professional points route

Japan's Highly Skilled Professional (HSP) status uses a points system. It has three categories:

1. Advanced Academic Research Activities.
2. Advanced Specialized / Technical Activities.
3. Advanced Business / Management Activities.

For this candidate, the most relevant categories are:

- **Advanced Specialized / Technical Activities** for technical services, reliability, production systems, engineering operations, manufacturing excellence, SAP/MRP/CMMS, and project execution roles.
- **Advanced Business / Management Activities** only if the candidate is hired into a genuine business management/executive role.

Core rule:

- the candidate must meet the underlying work-status requirements and score at least 70 points.

The points calculation considers factors such as:

- academic background;
- professional career/practical experience;
- annual salary;
- age;
- research or professional achievements;
- Japanese degrees or Japanese-language ability;
- bonus-point categories.

Why this route can help:

- HSP gives preferential treatment compared with ordinary work status.
- HSP 1 usually grants a 5-year period of stay.
- HSP has preferential processing, spouse work advantages, and a shortened permanent residence pathway.
- Permanent residence can become possible after 3 years for 70+ points, or after 1 year for 80+ points, subject to conditions.

Profile risk:

- Age points may be low or zero for a senior candidate.
- A high Japan salary is important to compensate for lost age points.
- Japanese language ability, recognised qualifications, and documented achievements can materially improve the score.

Practical recommendation:

Run the HSP points calculation after a serious offer is likely. Do not lead every employer conversation with HSP unless the salary and job level make it plausible. Use standard Engineer/Specialist status as the base plan and HSP as the upgrade path.

### Route 3: J-SKIP special highly skilled route

J-SKIP is Japan's special highly skilled professional route introduced in April 2023. It bypasses the normal points calculation if the candidate meets specific education/career and income thresholds.

For advanced academic research or advanced specialised/technical activities:

- master's degree or higher plus annual salary of at least JPY 20 million; or
- at least 10 years of practical experience related to the intended work plus annual salary of at least JPY 20 million.

For advanced business/management activities:

- at least 5 years of experience in business management or administration; and
- annual salary of at least JPY 40 million.

Benefits include:

- Highly Skilled Professional status;
- 5-year period of stay under HSP 1;
- expanded preferential treatment;
- potential move to HSP 2 after one year if conditions are met;
- strong permanent residence advantage.

Reality check:

- JPY 20 million is a high salary in Japan and is usually limited to executive, director-level, regional/global expert, or scarce specialist roles.
- JPY 40 million for business/management is very high and should not be assumed.
- This route is possible only if the candidate targets senior global roles, director-level manufacturing excellence, regional maintenance/reliability leadership, or highly paid industrial transformation roles.

Recommendation:

Treat J-SKIP as an aspirational route. It is not the baseline. If a multinational offers a senior role above JPY 20 million, evaluate it immediately.

### Route 4: Intra-company transferee

This route can apply where an employee is transferred to Japan from an overseas office of the same company or group.

Use case:

- Candidate joins a multinational employer outside Japan.
- The employer has a Japanese branch/subsidiary.
- After a qualifying period and business need, the employer transfers the candidate to Japan.

Why it matters:

- It can bypass some external-hire friction.
- Japanese companies are often more comfortable moving someone internally than sponsoring a cold overseas hire.

Limit:

- It is not immediate unless the candidate already works for a company with Japan operations.

### Route 5: Business Manager

This is for people who will manage or operate a business in Japan, not for ordinary employment.

It may require:

- a real business plan;
- capital or investment;
- office premises;
- governance and operational evidence;
- ability to show the business is viable and properly managed.

Recommendation:

Do not use this route unless the candidate has a serious Japan business plan, capital, local partners, and professional legal support.

### Route 6: Specified Skilled Worker

Specified Skilled Worker (SSW) covers designated fields including manufacture of industrial products and food/beverage manufacturing.

Typical requirements include:

- sector-specific skills evaluation;
- Japanese language ability such as JLPT N4 or JFT-Basic, depending on route and field;
- job offer from an accepting organisation;
- direct employment in the relevant field.

Why it is not ideal:

- It is designed for shop-floor skilled work, not senior management.
- SSW(i) is time-limited and does not generally provide the same family/long-term advantages as professional routes.
- It can under-position a senior candidate with 20+ years of leadership experience.

Recommendation:

Do not make SSW the primary pathway. Consider it only if the candidate deliberately wants to restart at a lower operational level, has Japanese language proof, and accepts the trade-offs.

## 4. Certificate of Eligibility and visa process

Japan work immigration is normally a two-step process.

### Step 1: Secure a Japanese sponsor

The candidate needs:

- a job offer or employment contract;
- a Japanese employer willing to sponsor;
- job duties that match the intended status of residence;
- documentation proving education, experience, and job relevance.

### Step 2: Employer applies for Certificate of Eligibility

The Certificate of Eligibility (COE) is issued by the Immigration Services Agency of Japan before visa application. It confirms that the planned activity fits a status of residence and meets landing criteria.

Important points:

- COE application is normally submitted in Japan by the receiving organisation or authorised representative.
- COE applications can be submitted online in some cases.
- There is no fee for COE issuance.
- Standard processing period is generally 1 to 3 months.
- Electronic COE can be received by email under the newer electronic COE system.
- Japanese certificates submitted in immigration procedures are generally expected to be recent, often within 3 months of issue.

### Step 3: Apply for visa in Nigeria after COE

For Nigeria:

- from March 2025, most Japan visa applications and passport collections are processed through Japan Visa Application Centres in Abuja and Lagos, operated by VFS Global;
- the Embassy of Japan in Nigeria generally accepts direct applications only for limited exceptions such as urgent humanitarian cases, diplomatic/official cases, or Government of Japan invitations;
- COE holders apply under the Work / Long Term Stay / Certificate of Eligibility category;
- applications are normally submitted in person;
- the Japan Visa Application Centre guidance indicates processing of about 12 working days for Abuja and about 14 working days for Lagos, assuming no issues;
- Lagos applications may involve courier transfer fees because processing is linked through Abuja.

Typical visa documents:

- valid passport;
- visa application form;
- photo;
- original or electronic COE evidence as accepted by the current procedure;
- employment contract/offer details if requested;
- CV and supporting documents if requested;
- visa fees and service charges;
- any additional documents required by JVAC or the Embassy.

### Step 4: Enter Japan and complete residence formalities

After arrival:

- receive residence card at port of entry if applicable;
- register address at municipal office after settling;
- join health insurance/pension systems as required;
- open bank account, phone, housing, and tax/social insurance setup;
- keep employer and immigration records consistent;
- notify authorities if changing employer/status where required.

## 7. Language strategy

Japan is much more language-sensitive than Ireland, Germany, or Finland for plant leadership roles.

### English-only possibilities

English-only or English-dominant roles are possible mainly in:

- foreign-affiliated multinationals;
- regional/global manufacturing excellence teams;
- pharma/life sciences project roles;
- semiconductor global teams;
- global SAP/MES/CMMS projects;
- external consulting/project roles;
- Tokyo-based corporate operations roles.

### Japanese expectations

For site-level leadership, Japanese is usually required because the role may involve:

- shop-floor operators;
- maintenance technicians;
- safety instructions;
- daily management meetings;
- vendor coordination;
- government/regulatory documents;
- union or HR communication;
- Japanese SOPs and equipment records.

### Recommended language plan

- Immediate: start Japanese language learning now.
- First target: JLPT N5/N4 for basic survival and SSW fallback awareness.
- Practical employability target: JLPT N3.
- Strong plant-leadership target: JLPT N2.
- Professional target for senior management: business Japanese plus technical vocabulary.

Also document English proficiency if possible, using IELTS, TOEFL, TOEIC, or employer-recognised evidence. Many Japan roles specify TOEIC-style English scores, especially in global companies.

## 9. Application strategy

### Best route first

Use three application tracks:

1. **Foreign-affiliated multinational track:** English-friendly roles in pharma, semiconductor, automotive, industrial automation, logistics, or manufacturing systems.
2. **Japan multinational global team track:** Japanese companies with global plants, overseas projects, and English interface.
3. **Recruiter-led track:** bilingual recruiters who understand visa sponsorship and can explain the candidate's fit to employers.

### Filtering rules

Prioritise roles where:

- employer is large, listed, multinational, or clearly experienced with foreign hires;
- role is professional/managerial, not manual factory labour;
- duties require specialised technical, engineering, business, or manufacturing-systems knowledge;
- English is accepted or the team is global;
- Japanese is preferred but not mandatory, or the candidate can commit to a learning plan;
- salary is senior enough to support HSP points if possible;
- employer can sponsor COE;
- job description includes SAP, CMMS, TPM, Lean, reliability, production planning, project management, validation, facilities, maintenance, or operational excellence.

Avoid roles where:

- "native Japanese" is mandatory;
- duties are primarily manual, assembly-line, operator, or shift technician work;
- employer is too small to sponsor confidently;
- salary is low or unclear;
- the listing says visa sponsorship is unavailable;
- job title is management but duties are mostly routine supervision without specialised knowledge.
