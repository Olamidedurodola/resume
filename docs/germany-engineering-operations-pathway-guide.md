# Germany Pathway Guide for a Senior Engineering and Operations Candidate

Prepared for: Confidential senior engineering and operations candidate
Current location assumption: Nigeria
Target country: Germany
Research date: 31 May 2026

> This guide is practical job-search and relocation preparation material, not legal advice. German immigration rules, salary thresholds, embassy requirements, and recognition procedures can change. Before submitting an application, verify the current requirements on Make it in Germany, the Federal Foreign Office, the German mission in Nigeria, Anabin, ZAB, and the Recognition in Germany portal.

## 1. Executive summary

Germany is a strong target for this candidate, but the best route is not a generic job-seeker approach. The strongest strategy is to position the candidate as a senior manufacturing, maintenance reliability, technical services, SAP/MRP, TPM/IWS, Lean Six Sigma, and capital-project execution leader for German industrial employers.

The best routes are ranked as follows:

1. **EU Blue Card** - best if the candidate can prove a recognised/comparable academic or equivalent tertiary qualification and secure a qualifying German offer. This is the premium route because it has faster settlement, easier family benefits, and strong employer recognition.
2. **Work visa for qualified professionals, Section 18a/18b Residence Act** - strong if the candidate's academic or vocational qualification is recognised/comparable and the offer is a qualified job. This route may be more flexible than the Blue Card where the job is not a perfect academic-degree match.
3. **Practical professional knowledge route** - important fallback for a highly experienced candidate in non-regulated industrial roles if formal recognition is difficult, provided the job offer and salary meet the rules.
4. **Recognition partnership** - useful where a German employer wants the candidate but the qualification-recognition process must be completed after entry.
5. **Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)** - a secondary job-search route, not the first choice for a senior candidate, because it requires funds, points, and a move before a job is secured.

The candidate should target Germany from Nigeria with a job-offer-first campaign. The Opportunity Card should only be used if the candidate has strong funds, at least B2 English or A1 German, a credible qualification file, and a focused in-country job-search plan.

## 3. Germany immigration pathway ranking

### Route 1: EU Blue Card

The EU Blue Card is Germany's main route for highly qualified non-EU professionals.

Official 2026 requirements from Make it in Germany include:

- a specific job offer from a German employer;
- employment period of at least six months;
- job must match the academic degree or comparable qualification;
- gross annual salary of at least EUR 50,700 in 2026 for the standard route;
- lower gross annual salary of at least EUR 45,934.20 in 2026 for shortage occupations, subject to Federal Employment Agency approval;
- qualification must be recognised/comparable in Germany, or an equivalent tertiary qualification must be accepted.

Shortage occupation relevance:

Germany's expanded shortage list is highly relevant. It includes:

- manufacturing, mining, construction, and distribution managers;
- manufacturing managers;
- supply, distribution, and related managers;
- science and engineering professionals;
- industrial and production engineers;
- mechanical engineers;
- electrical/electronics engineers;
- engineering professionals not elsewhere classified.

Why this route can work:

- The profile aligns strongly with manufacturing management, maintenance/reliability leadership, production/process improvement, and industrial operations.
- Germany's shortage list explicitly includes manufacturing managers and engineering professionals.
- Senior German industrial roles can meet or exceed the 2026 salary thresholds.
- EU Blue Card offers a strong settlement pathway: settlement can be possible after 27 months with A1 German, or after 21 months with B1 German, subject to conditions.

Main risk:

- The job must match the academic or equivalent tertiary qualification. If the recognised qualification is business/operations-focused, the Blue Card case may be stronger for manufacturing operations, production management, supply/distribution management, or technical services leadership than for a pure "mechanical engineer" title.
- If an engineering degree is not yet recognised/comparable, the candidate should not rely only on a mechanical-engineer Blue Card strategy.

Best Blue Card job titles for this profile:

- Manufacturing Manager
- Senior Manufacturing Manager
- Production Manager / Produktionsleiter
- Operations Excellence Manager
- Technical Services Manager
- Engineering Operations Manager
- Maintenance Reliability Manager
- Global Maintenance Manager
- Project Manager, Manufacturing or Engineering
- Industrial/Production Engineering Manager
- Supply, Distribution or Production Planning Manager, if the role is sufficiently qualified and technical

### Route 2: Work visa for qualified professionals, Section 18a/18b

This route applies to qualified professionals with recognised vocational training or a recognised/comparable academic degree.

Official requirements include:

- recognised/comparable academic degree or qualified vocational training;
- specific job offer from a German employer;
- the role must be a qualified position, not auxiliary work;
- the job does not always have to be directly related to the qualification, except where a regulated profession requires a licence;
- Federal Employment Agency approval is normally required to confirm comparable employment conditions;
- if the candidate is over 45 and entering Germany for work for the first time, the job must pay at least EUR 55,770 gross per year in 2026, or the candidate must prove adequate pension provision.

Why this route may be very useful:

- The candidate has a broad operations/business/technical profile. If a recognised academic qualification is confirmed, Section 18b may be more flexible than a Blue Card where the job title is not a perfect degree match.
- Many target jobs are qualified management or specialist roles but may not be clean "engineer" titles.
- This route can be used for roles such as technical services, operations excellence, production management, maintenance leadership, and SAP/MRP planning where the employer values experience plus recognised qualifications.

Best use case:

- Employer offers a qualified management or technical role.
- The salary is competitive but either below the EU Blue Card threshold or the degree/job match is not ideal for Blue Card.
- Qualification recognition/comparability can be documented.

### Route 3: Practical professional knowledge

Germany's Skilled Immigration Act expanded access for people with highly developed practical professional knowledge in non-regulated professions.

Official Make it in Germany guidance states that, for this route:

- formal German recognition of the qualification is not required;
- the candidate needs a non-academic vocational qualification or academic qualification recognised by the country of training;
- non-academic vocational training should normally have taken at least two years;
- the candidate needs at least two years of work experience in the occupation;
- the job offer must pay at least EUR 45,934.20 gross per year in 2026, unless collective-agreement pay rules apply;
- the role must be in a non-regulated profession.

Why this route matters:

- The candidate has deep practical experience in industrial operations, maintenance, production, reliability, SAP/MRP, and project execution.
- It may help if degree equivalence is slow or unclear.
- It can be useful for technical operations, maintenance leadership, production leadership, and industrial project roles that are not regulated professions.

Main caution:

- This route still requires a strong employer, a concrete job offer, and a salary that satisfies the rules.
- Employers may be less familiar with this pathway than the EU Blue Card, so the candidate may need to explain it clearly and point employers to Make it in Germany.

### Route 4: Recognition partnership

The recognition partnership allows a candidate to enter Germany and work while completing professional recognition with employer support.

Official requirements include:

- foreign academic qualification or vocational qualification requiring at least two years of full-time training;
- qualification must be recognised by the government of the country where it was obtained;
- concrete German job offer for qualified employment;
- written recognition partnership agreement with the employer;
- German language skills at A2 or above;
- suitable employer with experience in vocational training or skills development;
- Federal Employment Agency approval of employment conditions.

Why it matters:

- This can solve a common problem: a German employer likes the candidate, but recognition is not complete before entry.
- The candidate can work while the recognition process is completed.
- The residence title is initially up to 12 months and may be extended in one-year steps up to a maximum of three years while recognition is pursued.

Best use case:

- Employer wants to hire but qualification status is not fully settled.
- Candidate has at least A2 German and is willing to complete recognition/adaptation steps in Germany.
- Employer has HR capability and is willing to support the process.

### Route 5: Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte)

The Opportunity Card allows qualified non-EU workers to enter Germany for up to one year to look for work.

Official requirements include either:

1. full recognition of a foreign vocational or academic qualification; or
2. at least six points in the points system.

Other core requirements:

- proof of sufficient funds, with the 2026 blocked-account benchmark of EUR 1,091 net per month;
- for the points route, proof of formal academic/vocational qualification and language skills;
- A1 German or B2 English as a baseline language requirement for applicants using the points route;
- the card allows part-time work up to 20 hours per week while searching;
- trial employment is allowed for up to two weeks per employer where the trial aims at qualified employment, apprenticeship, or qualification measures.

Likely points considerations for this candidate:

- Professional experience: likely strong.
- Shortage occupation: likely possible if qualification/target field maps to engineering/manufacturing/technical management.
- English: likely useful if C1 can be proven, though the baseline is B2 for the points route.
- German: important; A2/B1/B2 adds points and improves employability.
- Age: if over 40, the candidate likely receives no age points.
- Partial recognition: if obtained, this can be powerful because partial recognition may carry significant points.

Recommendation:

Use the Opportunity Card only as a secondary route. It is useful if the candidate wants to physically enter Germany for interviews, but the job-offer-first route is safer for a senior professional with family and relocation considerations.

## 4. Qualification recognition strategy

Germany is strict about qualification evidence. The candidate should create a recognition file before applying heavily.

### Academic qualifications

For academic degrees:

1. Check the institution and degree in the Anabin database.
2. The institution should ideally be listed as H+.
3. The degree should be assessed as corresponding or equivalent to a German qualification.
4. If Anabin does not clearly confirm both institution and degree, apply to ZAB for a Statement of Comparability.

The ZAB Statement of Comparability:

- is an official certificate comparing a foreign university degree to the German education system;
- can be used with embassies, employers, and authorities;
- is especially important for EU Blue Card and qualified professional visa applications;
- may be processed faster for EU Blue Card cases when the candidate provides a work contract or written confirmation of employment.

### Vocational and technical qualifications

For technical diplomas and vocational-level credentials:

1. Use the Recognition in Germany portal to identify the competent authority.
2. Confirm whether the role is regulated or non-regulated.
3. For non-regulated industrial roles, full recognition may not always be mandatory, but it can still improve employer confidence.
4. For recognition partnership, the qualification must be recognised in the country of training and the employer must support the process in writing.

### Practical recommendation for this candidate

Prepare three qualification narratives:

1. **Academic management route:** recognised business/operations/MBA-level profile for manufacturing management, operations excellence, production leadership, and technical services management.
2. **Technical operations route:** mechanical engineering diplomas plus industrial/production management and 20+ years of technical services/maintenance reliability experience.
3. **Future engineering-degree route:** if the in-progress industrial engineering master's is completed and recognised, it can strengthen Blue Card alignment for industrial/production engineering leadership.

Do not wait passively for perfect recognition before job searching. Start applications while preparing Anabin/ZAB evidence, but be honest with employers that recognition documentation is being prepared.

## 11. Application strategy

### Best route first

Use a two-track application strategy:

1. **Premium track:** EU Blue Card-suitable roles with salary above EUR 45,934.20 or EUR 50,700 and a clear degree/qualification match.
2. **Practical track:** qualified professional, practical professional knowledge, or recognition partnership roles where the employer values deep experience and can support the visa process.

### Application filtering

Prioritise jobs where:

- salary is likely above EUR 55,770 if the candidate is over 45, or at least high enough for Blue Card/practical knowledge thresholds;
- English is accepted or German is listed as preferred rather than mandatory;
- role is senior, qualified, and not auxiliary;
- job responsibilities include maintenance strategy, reliability, SAP PM/EAM/MRP, TPM, Lean, production planning, capital projects, safety, compliance, or plant improvement;
- company is multinational or has HR experience with non-EU skilled workers;
- employer is willing to support qualification recognition or recognition partnership if needed.

Avoid:

- low-level technician roles requiring only German vocational qualifications;
- shift-only maintenance technician roles unless the employer explicitly supports recognition/visa;
- roles requiring native/fluent German where the candidate does not yet have it;
- roles below salary threshold;
- contractor offers without clear employment, visa, salary, and job-description documents.

## 15. Offer and visa checklist

Before accepting a German offer, confirm:

- legal employer name and address;
- job title;
- full job description;
- contract duration;
- start date;
- gross annual salary;
- weekly working hours;
- probation period;
- location and travel requirements;
- collective agreement status, if any;
- whether German or English is the working language;
- whether the employer supports EU Blue Card, qualified professional visa, practical professional knowledge route, or recognition partnership;
- whether employer will provide Declaration of Employment;
- whether employer will use accelerated skilled-worker procedure;
- relocation support, temporary housing, flights, family support, and visa-cost support;
- whether qualification recognition is required before entry or can be completed after arrival.

For Blue Card:

- salary meets 2026 threshold;
- contract is at least six months;
- job matches recognised/comparable qualification;
- Anabin/ZAB proof is ready;
- shortage occupation mapping is clear if using the lower threshold.

For qualified professional route:

- qualification is recognised/comparable;
- job is qualified, not auxiliary;
- if over 45, salary is at least EUR 55,770 in 2026 or pension provision is documented.

For recognition partnership:

- A2 German proof is ready;
- written recognition partnership agreement is in the contract or separate document;
- employer is suitable and willing to support recognition;
- qualified job offer is clear.

## 16. Practical action plan

1. Build a Germany-specific CV and one-page skills profile.
2. Start Anabin checks for all academic degrees.
3. If Anabin is unclear, prepare a ZAB Statement of Comparability application.
4. Use Recognition in Germany to map technical diplomas and identify competent authorities.
5. Start German language learning immediately; target A2 first, then B1/B2.
6. Build a quantified achievement bank: downtime, OEE, MTBF, MTTR, SAP/MRP, projects, team size, cost savings, safety.
7. Create a target list of 75 German employers across FMCG, food/beverage, pharma, chemicals, automotive, logistics automation, and industrial engineering.
8. Apply first to English-friendly multinational roles.
9. Message recruiters and hiring managers for every strong application.
10. Keep a tracker showing job title, company, location, salary if known, language requirement, visa route fit, recognition requirement, and follow-up date.
11. Prepare visa documents before interviews reach offer stage.
12. Before accepting any offer, test it against Blue Card, qualified professional, practical knowledge, or recognition partnership requirements.
